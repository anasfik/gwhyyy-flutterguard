What it reads from the APK

Static evidence, grouped by where it hurts.

Flutter fingerprint

Flutter or not, AOT or JIT, engine version, obfuscation, and a fingerprint for desktop follow-up.

Manifest risk

Permissions, exported components, debug flags, backup, cleartext policy, and deep links.

Secrets and endpoints

Keys, JWTs, DB URIs, .env files, X.509 blobs, and hosts classified by role.

SDK and AI surface

About 135 services, from ads to auth, with LLM providers inventoried individually.

Artifact and signing

ABIs, sizes, hashes, schemes, signer identity, and debug certificate detection.

A, C, and F are the anchors

What it does not do

Reads only what the APK exposes, and nothing more.

  • Artifact reads only

    No runtime hooking, no traffic interception, no other apps' data.

  • No decompiled Dart

    libapp.so is AOT machine code. You get strings, symbols and routes, not source.

  • Routes are candidates

    Recovered route strings are grouped by prefix, not a navigation graph.

  • AABs need bundletool

    Expand an AAB to APKs first. The app reads APK files.

  • Signature checks are structural

    Schemes and signer identity, not a full apksigner verification.

  • Some ROMs block APK reads

    That is an OS policy on the device, not a FlutterGuard setting.

Scan only apps you own or have written permission to test.

Before you install.

Something missing? Ask directly.

Is it free?

Yes. MIT licensed, no account, no ads, no analytics.

Does it really work offline?

Yes. The app has no INTERNET permission. Scans run on the phone.

Is it Android only?

Yes. Android 8.0+ (API 26). There is no iOS build.

Does it prove vulnerabilities?

No. Static findings are leads to investigate, not proof. Read the evidence and verify.

Why is it not on Google Play yet?

The Play release is in review. The GitHub APK is the current build.

Does uninstalling delete my reports?

Yes. Reports, history and settings live in private app storage and go with the uninstall. Export first.
